# Language Learning Context

## Purpose

Explains the pedagogical approach and research basis for Babblr.

## Learning Philosophy

### Communicative Language Teaching (CLT)
- Focus on meaningful communication
- Real-world tasks over drills
- Fluency and accuracy balanced

### Comprehensible Input (Krashen's i+1)
- Learning occurs when exposed to language slightly above current level
- CEFR levels implement this principle

### Error Correction Strategy
- Gentle, explanatory corrections
- Focus on meaning first, accuracy second
- Positive reinforcement
- A recast gives the correct form back to the learner. Learners often do not notice a recast and do not repair the error. A prompt (for example, a hint or a question) holds back the correct form and lets the learner correct the error themselves. Prompts cause more learner repair (Lyster & Ranta, 1997). Use prompts when the learner probably knows the correct form. Use recasts for forms that the learner has not learned yet.

### Durable Learning (Desirable Difficulties)
- Each memory has two strengths. Storage strength is how well the learner has learned the item. Retrieval strength is how easily the learner can recall it now. Easy recall during practice can hide weak storage (Bjork & Bjork, 1992).
- Recall from memory (retrieval practice) gives better retention after days and weeks than more study of the same material (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006).
- Practice spread over time (spacing) gives better retention than the same practice in one block. When the learner must remember for a longer time, the best gap between practice sessions is also longer (Cepeda et al., 2006).
- Difficulty helps practice, but it does not help a first explanation. Keep new material easy to understand. Make practice of known material effortful.

## Research Basis

- Krashen, S. (1982). Principles and Practice in Second Language Acquisition
- Swain, M. (1985). Communicative competence: Some roles of comprehensible input and comprehensible output
- Ellis, R. (2003). Task-based Language Learning and Teaching
- Bjork, R. A., & Bjork, E. L. (1992). A new theory of disuse and an old theory of stimulus fluctuation. In A. Healy, S. Kosslyn, & R. Shiffrin (Eds.), *From Learning Processes to Cognitive Processes: Essays in Honor of William K. Estes* (Vol. 2, pp. 35-67)
- Lyster, R., & Ranta, L. (1997). Corrective feedback and learner uptake: Negotiation of form in communicative classrooms. *Studies in Second Language Acquisition*, 19, 37-66
- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science*, 17(3), 249-255. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x
- Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis. *Psychological Bulletin*, 132(3), 354-380. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354

## Implementation in Babblr

- **Natural Conversation**: CLT approach
- **CEFR Levels**: Comprehensible input (i+1)
- **Error Corrections**: Research-backed gentle corrections
- **Topic-Based**: Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT)
- **Spacing**: Grammar review dates grow with mastery score (`calculate_next_review_date` in `backend/app/routes/grammar.py`)
